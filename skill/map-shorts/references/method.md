# Method: what makes a map Short hold viewers

Distilled from running a Thai map-explainer channel (about 13 clips, 2 per day) and studying competitors. Treat as strong hints, test on your own audience.

## Reading the numbers
- Judge a clip on **day 3**, not hour 1. Shorts often get a delayed second wave.
- On a clip under ~50 s, **average view about 100%** (people watch it all, loops) is the best sign. "Stayed to watch" 60-75% is healthy for a cold feed.
- Follower conversion from the Shorts feed is tiny (about 0.06%). Do not expect views to turn into followers; expect them to turn into reach. Do not add a "please follow" outro: it cost retention and gained nothing.

## Topic formula (all three, if possible)
1. **Stakes for the viewer** - "which province floods first", "is the volcano near my home dead". Local pride and fear beat trivia.
2. **A common wrong belief corrected** - "the longest river does not reach the sea", "Thailand has volcanoes".
3. **A visual only a 3D map gives** - terrain, a basin, distance, a line across a region. If a still photo would do, pick another topic.
Niche foreign places and pure trivia do worst. Broad "why" topics about the viewer's own country do best.

## Script
- Line 1 = the surprising claim, spoken in the first 2 s, also on screen as a title. No greeting, no channel name.
- 4-7 lines. One idea per line. Every line gets a new visual event (camera move, card, counter, pill).
- Spoken register, short sentences, numbers as a person says them ("about nine hundred thousand years ago").
- Attribute claims that could be disputed to a named source ("a Chulalongkorn geologist says...").
- End with a question the comment section can answer ("which volcano is nearest you?"). Pin your own answer as the first comment.

## Pacing (the retention rules)
- **Change the frame every 3-4 s.** A still frame for 6 s is where people leave. Alternate: wide -> close-up -> overlay appears -> camera moves.
- Energy comes from UI pops, counters and sound, not from camera churn. Keep the camera smooth; never zoom back and forth.
- Hold each card long enough to read it (about 2 s minimum, 3 s for two lines).
- Safe zones: top ~210 px and bottom ~310 px are covered by platform UI. Titles at y~240, captions at y~1390.

## Sound
- One narrator voice, steady speed (1.0-1.06). Faster than ~1.1 sounds rushed.
- Sound effects by *meaning* only (a pop when a card lands, a whoosh on a big camera move). No effect on every cut.
- Music quiet and ducked under the voice, or none.

## Honesty
- Verify each number against two sources. Say "about" where it is approximate.
- Illustrations (plumes, rings, arcs) are graphics, not data; say so in the credits.
- If a platform asks about AI-generated content: map graphics and synthetic narration are generally "not realistic footage of real events"; read the current platform rules and answer truthfully.
